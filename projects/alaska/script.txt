This giant piece of land was once Russian. Then, in 1867, Russia sold it to the United States for just seven point two million dollars. That's about two cents an acre. So why would an empire give away land this big?

You see, Russia had just lost the Crimean War, and its treasury was nearly empty. Alaska was huge, freezing and almost impossible to defend. Only a few hundred Russians lived there, and the sea otters they hunted for fur were nearly gone.

Even worse, right next door sat British Canada. If war broke out again, Britain could simply take Alaska for free.

So Tsar Alexander the Second decided to sell it first, to Britain's rival.

His envoy, Eduard de Stoeckl, found a buyer in Secretary of State William Seward. And on October eighteenth, 1867, the Russian flag came down in Sitka.

But back home, Americans laughed. They called it Seward's Folly. Seward's Icebox.

Here's the twist. Gold rushes followed. Then in 1968, oil was struck at Prudhoe Bay. Today Alaska is America's largest state, bigger than Texas, California and Montana combined.

Two cents an acre, for the biggest bargain on the map.
